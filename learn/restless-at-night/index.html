Restless at night

Sort pacing, wake-ups, toileting, thirst, breathing, and disorientation into a timeline your veterinarian can use.

Reviewed starting source: Merck Veterinary Manual ↗
In this lesson01Name the exact wake-up02Check the simple needs03Build the right question!When to call sooner
CHAPTER 01

Name the exact wake-up

Write down the time, what happened first, where your dog went, and what finally helped them settle.

  • Record pacing, panting, vocalizing, scratching, or asking to go out separately.
  • Note medication, meals, visitors, weather, and exercise that day.
  • Log several nights before looking for a pattern.
CHAPTER 02

Check the simple needs

Make the sleep environment easy to navigate without changing five things at once.

  • Keep water and a clear toileting route available.
  • Use night lighting and traction on the usual path.
  • Check temperature, noise, and access to a familiar rest spot.
CHAPTER 03

Build the right question

Night changes can have many causes. Share the timeline rather than trying to diagnose it at home.

  • Ask whether pain, bathroom urgency, medication timing, vision, hearing, or cognitive changes should be evaluated.
  • Bring short clips only when they capture the ordinary behavior safely.
  • Ask what should trigger after-hours help.
CALL SOONER WHEN

Do not wait for a perfect record.

Breathing difficulty, repeated unproductive retching, collapse, a swollen abdomen, inability to urinate, severe distress, or sudden confusion needs urgent veterinary advice.

Educational boundary

This lesson provides general education, not a diagnosis or individual treatment plan. A veterinarian who examines your dog should guide medical decisions.
