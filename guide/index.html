The complete Senior Dog Care Guide

Understand the changes aging dogs face. Know what to check, what to make easier today, and what deserves a faster call.

Send the guide to my inbox

Movement, sleep, eating, drinking, bathroom changes and daily life—in one practical guide.

1

Recognize the exact change.

Replace vague worry with a specific behavior, time and routine.

2

Make today easier.

Use low-risk changes that protect access, traction, rest and dignity.

3

Know when to call.

Separate useful tracking from signs that should not wait.

Six changes. Three decisions for each.

What to watch, what to do today and when to call sooner.

Movement

Watch
Watch the first five steps after rest, slipping, stairs and recovery.
Do today
Add traction, shorten difficult routes and record one natural rise.
Call sooner
Sudden inability to stand, dragging a limb or severe pain.

Sleep

Watch
Record wake time, pacing, breathing, toileting and what helps settling.
Do today
Keep water, a clear bathroom route and gentle night lighting available.
Call sooner
Breathing difficulty, collapse or severe disorientation.

Eating

Watch
Measure what was offered and eaten; note chewing, nausea and weight.
Do today
Keep food familiar and bowls easy to reach while arranging care.
Call sooner
Repeated vomiting, a swollen abdomen or refusing all food with weakness.

Water

Watch
Measure one ordinary day; pair thirst with urine, appetite and medicines.
Do today
Keep fresh water available and add easier bathroom access.
Call sooner
Inability to urinate, collapse or severe distress.

Bathroom

Watch
Record time, urgency, posture, amount, route and signs of pain.
Do today
Offer more frequent access on a short, well-lit, non-slip route.
Call sooner
Repeated straining without output, blood with weakness or severe pain.

Daily life

Watch
Name the exact routine your dog no longer starts, joins or finishes.
Do today
Offer a shorter, lower-effort version and let your dog choose.
Call sooner
Sudden confusion, seizure, collapse or rapid major behavior change.

Your seven-day change record

  1. Day 1 — Set the baseline. Film or note one ordinary routine without prompting.
  2. Days 2–3 — Repeat at the same time. Look for frequency, effort and recovery.
  3. Days 4–5 — Change one safe detail. Add traction, access or a shorter route; record the result.
  4. Day 6 — Connect the systems. Add sleep, appetite, water, bathroom and medicines.
  5. Day 7 — Write the summary. State what changed, how often, what helped and what is harder now.

Bring a better first sentence to the appointment.

“For ten days, she pauses after naps, has slipped twice in the hallway, and now avoids the two kitchen steps. A runner helps, but the change is still happening daily.”
  • Bring the timeline and short natural videos.
  • List medicines, supplements and recent changes.
  • Ask which causes need evaluation and what to monitor next.

Built with veterinarian-supported observation principles.

Notice the pattern, make the day safer and bring a clearer story to the professional who knows your dog.
