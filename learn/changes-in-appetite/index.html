Changes in appetite

Record amount, timing, weight, nausea clues, and interest in favorite foods without guessing at a cause.

Reviewed starting source: Merck Veterinary Manual ↗

CHAPTER 01

Measure what changed

‘Eating less’ becomes more useful when the amount and timing are clear.

  • Record what was offered and what was actually eaten.
  • Add treats, water, vomiting, stool, medicines, and a recent weight when available.
  • Use the same measuring method each day.
CHAPTER 02

Notice how eating looks

The way your dog approaches food can add important context.

  • Note dropping food, chewing on one side, or wanting only soft food.
  • Record lip licking, repeated swallowing, approaching then walking away, or hiding.
  • Do not add supplements or make a restrictive diet change without veterinary guidance.
CHAPTER 03

Bring a one-page summary

Keep the conversation focused on the change, the timeline, and your dog’s whole routine.

  • List dental history, medical conditions, and current medications.
  • Ask how soon your dog should be examined.
  • Confirm what food, water, weight, and bathroom changes to monitor next.
CALL SOONER WHEN

Do not wait for a perfect record.

Repeated vomiting, abdominal swelling, marked weakness, blood, pain, dehydration, known diabetes with poor appetite, or a pet who stops eating needs prompt veterinary advice.

Educational boundary

This lesson provides general education, not a diagnosis or individual treatment plan. A veterinarian who examines your dog should guide medical decisions.
