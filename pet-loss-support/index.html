A PRACTICAL PATH

Six steps, taken at your pace.

You can start anywhere. If your dog is struggling now, contact a veterinary team before completing a worksheet.

Urgent changes

Breathing trouble, collapse, uncontrolled pain, repeated seizures, severe bleeding, a swollen abdomen with distress, or inability to urinate needs immediate veterinary help.

01

Record today’s reality

Write appetite, water, breathing, mobility, sleep, bathroom comfort, anxiety, and the routines your dog still enjoys. Note what changed and when.

02

Call the right team

Ask your primary veterinarian who handles urgent changes, after-hours questions, hospice, home visits, and euthanasia planning. Save the numbers where the family can find them.

03

Make a one-week comfort plan

Confirm medicines, food and water access, toileting, safe movement, sleep, anxiety, and the exact signs that mean the plan needs review.

04

Prepare for the appointment

Ask what will happen before, during, and after; who may be present; whether home or clinic care is available; and what your family wants to bring.

05

Confirm aftercare without pressure

Discuss transport, identity handling, cremation or burial options, return timing, costs, and whether you want a paw print, fur clipping, or private time.

06

Protect the first 72 hours

Choose who will tell others, pause nonessential decisions, plan for children or other animals, keep simple food and support nearby, and know who to call if grief feels unsafe.

WORDS FOR THE HARD CALL

You can read this aloud.

Specific language can help when emotion makes it hard to begin.

“My dog’s comfort has changed. I need help understanding what is urgent, what can be made easier today, and what choices we may need to prepare for.”

Questions to ask

  • What are you seeing that suggests comfort is changing?
  • What can we do today, and how will we know it helps?
  • Which signs mean we should call tonight?
  • Who can support us at home or after hours?
  • What will happen if we choose an appointment?

Grief support should be qualified and human.

WoafyPet does not match grieving strangers as peer counselors and does not provide crisis counseling. Use credentialed grief support, your health professional, or emergency services when safety is at risk.
