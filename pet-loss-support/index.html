Make today gentler.

  1. Write today’s reality.Food, water, breathing, mobility, sleep, bathroom comfort, anxiety, and the routines still enjoyed.
  2. Call the right team.Save the primary, after-hours, hospice, and home-visit numbers where everyone can reach them.
  3. Make a one-week comfort plan.Confirm medicine, meals, toileting, safe movement, sleep, anxiety, and the signs that trigger a recheck.
Find hospice and comfort care →

Know what will happen before the day arrives.

  1. Ask about the appointment.Where it can happen, who may be present, how comfort is protected, and what your family may bring.
  2. Choose aftercare without pressure.Transport, identity handling, cremation or burial, return timing, cost, paw print, fur clipping, and private time.
  3. Decide who will carry each task.One person for the clinic, one for family updates, and one for children or other animals.

Words for the call when words are hard.

“My dog’s comfort has changed. Please help me understand what can be made easier today, what may be urgent, and what choices we should prepare for.”
  • What tells you comfort is changing?
  • What can we do today, and how will we know it helped?
  • Which signs mean call tonight?
  • What happens before, during, and after the appointment?

Protect the first 72 hours.

  1. Pause decisions that can wait.Food, rest, and one trusted person matter more than answering every message.
  2. Give grief somewhere to go.Write one memory, keep one familiar object nearby, or tell the story to someone who will listen.
  3. Reach for real support.Use a pet-loss counselor, veterinary social worker, group, or trusted clinician when the weight feels too heavy.
