Start with the change you can see.

Slower to stand or walk

Time one natural rise. Note the floor, the pause, the first 15 steps, and whether movement loosens. Add traction to the route your dog already uses.

Open the mobility lesson →

Waking or pacing at night

Record the exact time, first behavior, bathroom trip, breathing, and what finally helps. Keep water and the usual route easy to reach.

Open the nighttime lesson →

Eating or drinking differently

Measure what you offer and what remains. Add chewing, vomiting, stool, water, energy, and medicine timing.

Open the eating lesson →

Accidents or less interest in life

Record frequency, posture, and one familiar activity your dog starts, finishes, or avoids.

Open the daily-life lesson →

Bring this to your veterinarian—not “he seems off.”

What changed

After his morning nap, he paused 8 seconds before standing.

What happened next

He slipped twice on the hallway floor and loosened after 12 steps.

What to ask

What should we examine, and which change should make me call sooner?

Some changes cannot wait.

Do not wait for the guide if your dog has trouble breathing, collapses, repeatedly retches without bringing anything up, cannot urinate, has a seizure, suddenly cannot stand, or is in severe distress. Contact urgent veterinary care now.
