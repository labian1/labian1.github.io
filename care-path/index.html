Need urgent help?

Breathing trouble, collapse, repeated unproductive retching, inability to urinate, seizure, sudden inability to stand, or severe distress needs immediate veterinary care.

Open emergency guidance →
CHAPTER 01 · OBSERVE

Name the exact change

Turn “something feels off” into a calm record of what happened, when it began, and how it differs from your dog’s normal.

  1. Choose one ordinary moment.Watch a normal rise, meal, night wake-up, water refill, or bathroom trip without provoking the change.
  2. Add timing and context.Note the time, surface, previous activity, medication, food, weather, and what happened first.
  3. Compare the pattern.Record frequency, duration, what helps, and whether it is stable, improving, or becoming more frequent.

CHAPTER 02 · SUPPORT

Reduce friction today

Make the routine safer and easier while preserving useful context for the veterinary team.

  • Add traction and clear familiar routes.
  • Keep water, food, toileting, and rest easy to reach.
  • Change one environmental detail at a time so you can tell what helped.
  • Never give human medication, restrict water, or change prescribed treatment without veterinary guidance.
Do not stage symptoms for a video.One natural, safely captured moment is more useful than repeated testing that tires or frightens your dog.
CHAPTER 03 · ACT

Make the veterinary call specific

Bring a short timeline, the whole routine, and questions that end with a measurable follow-up plan.

  1. Lead with the change.Share when it began, how often it happens, what is different from normal, and what makes it better or worse.
  2. Bring the complete context.List medicines, supplements, food, water, bathroom changes, recent events, and one or two safe videos.
  3. Leave knowing what comes next.Ask what should be evaluated, what improvement should look like, and which signs mean you should call sooner.
