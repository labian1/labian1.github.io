Slower after rest

Turn a vague ‘slower morning’ into a calm, useful record of the first steps that matter.

Reviewed starting source: Merck Veterinary Manual ↗
In this lesson01Watch the transition02Change the setting, not the dog03Prepare the veterinary conversation!When to call sooner
CHAPTER 01

Watch the transition

Record the pause before standing, whether the back feet slip, how many steps look stiff, and whether movement becomes easier after one minute.

  • Observe one ordinary rise after a familiar nap.
  • Note the surface and time of day.
  • Compare the pattern across several days—not repeated tests in one sitting.
CHAPTER 02

Change the setting, not the dog

Small home adjustments can make ordinary movement safer while you collect a clearer picture for your veterinary team.

  • Keep the route clear and add traction where feet slide.
  • Place water and a comfortable rest area within easy reach.
  • Film one normal rise in good light; never force stairs or jumps for a video.
CHAPTER 03

Prepare the veterinary conversation

Bring a short timeline: when it began, what is changing, what helps, and which daily activities have become harder.

  • List current medicines and supplements.
  • Ask which changes need same-day advice.
  • Agree on what improvement should look like in daily life.
CALL SOONER WHEN

Do not wait for a perfect record.

Seek prompt veterinary help for sudden inability to stand, dragging a limb, crying out, collapse, severe swelling, or a rapid decline after an injury.

Educational boundary

This lesson provides general education, not a diagnosis or individual treatment plan. A veterinarian who examines your dog should guide medical decisions.
