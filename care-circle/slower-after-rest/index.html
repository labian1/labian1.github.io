Slower after rest

Turn a vague slower morning into a useful first-rise record and a safer plan for today.

Dog
Milo · 12 years · Golden Retriever
Owner-shared conditions
arthritis
What changed
slower first steps after naps; hallway slipping

A focused answer for Milo

Record the first rise clearly enough to compare again tomorrow.

Measure one natural first rise

Record the first rise clearly enough to compare again tomorrow.

Watch one ordinary rise after a familiar nap. Time the pause, count the first stiff steps, and note whether the feet slip or movement loosens.

  1. Use the same rest place and time of day when possible.
  2. Record the surface, pause, first 10–15 steps, and recovery.
  3. Never repeat stairs, jumps, or standing tests for a video.
Which note is most useful?

Make the usual route easier today

You will know which low-risk home change to try first.

Reduce slipping and unnecessary effort while keeping the routine familiar enough to see what actually helps.

  1. Add traction on the normal rise and water route.
  2. Keep water, toileting, and a comfortable rest place easy to reach.
  3. Change one environmental detail at a time and record the result.
What is the safest first change for slipping feet?

Build a vet-ready mobility summary

You will have the exact points to bring to the appointment.

Lead with when the change began, how often it happens, what helps, and which daily activity has become harder.

  1. List current medicines, supplements, injuries, and recent activity changes.
  2. Bring one safe video of an ordinary rise when available.
  3. Ask what improvement should look like and which changes need same-day advice.
What belongs in the first sentence of the veterinary call?

Call sooner when you see this

Seek prompt veterinary help for sudden inability to stand, dragging a limb, crying out, collapse, severe swelling, or rapid decline after an injury.
