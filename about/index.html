The quiet changes were easy to explain away.

Bobby was Robert's Alaskan Malamute—a loved family dog, never a case study. His slower rises, longer recovery and preference for the cool floor made one thing clear: families need better language for the changes they feel before they can name.

At first, each change felt too small to stop the day over. Bobby still greeted his people, enjoyed a bright afternoon and found his familiar way of asking to be close.

But taken together, the moments told a story. Comfort mattered. So did a calmer record of what was changing, when it happened and what made the day easier.

WoafMeow exists to help families feel seen, understand the pattern and reach the right next step with less fear.

WoafyPet grew from the same lesson: a bed should support the body first, while quiet technology helps the family notice repeated change without pretending to diagnose it.

Care before commerce

Education, grief support and human connection do not become product pressure.

Specific before dramatic

Useful observations and honest limits build more trust than alarming claims.

Human before automated

Technology should make a family calmer and more prepared—not more afraid.

Start with the change you noticed.

Clear guidance, credible support, and people who understand.
