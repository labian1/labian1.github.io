Chapter 01

Define the missing moment

You will identify one routine that changed instead of labeling your dog withdrawn.

Choose a familiar moment—greeting, sniff walk, mealtime, grooming, or resting near family—and compare what happens now.

  1. Record whether the dog starts, joins, finishes, or avoids the routine.
  2. Note time, environment, effort, and what helps engagement return.
  3. Look for paired movement, sleep, appetite, bathroom, hearing, or vision changes.
Which observation is clearest?

Chapter 02

Make connection easier

You will choose one lower-effort way to keep a valued routine available.

Reduce the effort required to join without forcing interaction.

  1. Bring the familiar rest place closer to family activity.
  2. Offer shorter sniff time, gentle grooming, or quiet contact based on preference.
  3. Stop if the dog turns away, stiffens, pants, hides, or seems uncomfortable.
What is the most respectful first step?

Chapter 03

Report the whole pattern

You will connect the behavior change to other daily functions for evaluation.

Interest changes can reflect many physical, sensory, cognitive, or emotional factors. Bring the pattern, not a label.

  1. Share onset, frequency, preferred routines, and paired physical changes.
  2. List medicines, recent events, hearing or vision changes, and sleep disruption.
  3. Ask which evaluations and home observations should come next.
