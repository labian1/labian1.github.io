FROM UNCERTAINTY TO A CLEARER PLAN

Know what to notice, what to do, and which solutions to discuss.

This is more than a record sheet. It explains common senior-dog problems, how to recognize a meaningful change, safer actions you can take today, existing care options, urgent boundaries, and how to leave a veterinary visit with a measurable follow-up plan.

WHAT TO WATCH

Five daily views—one whole dog.

A single score can hide the story. Look at patterns across ordinary functions.

Sleep & settling

Bed changes, pacing, wake-ups, panting, vocalizing, and what helps.

Movement

First rise, slipping, stairs, walk pace, turning, jumping, and recovery.

Food & water

Amounts, timing, interest, chewing, swallowing, nausea clues, and refills.

Bathroom

Frequency, effort, accidents, route, posture, stool, and urine changes.

Comfort & connection

Favorite routines, attention, restlessness, withdrawal, grooming, and joy.

DO NOT WAIT FOR A WORKSHEET

Some changes need immediate help.

Breathing trouble, collapse, repeated unproductive retching, inability to urinate, severe bleeding, seizures, sudden inability to stand, or severe distress needs urgent veterinary attention.

01

Be specific

“Paused 8 seconds before standing after the morning nap” is more useful than “seemed off.”

02

Keep life normal

Do not force stairs, jumps, meals, or repeated movements just to test a concern.

03

Record what helped

Note traction, a shorter walk, a quiet room, easier access, or another ordinary change.

04

Bring the timeline

Include when it began, how often it happens, medicines, recent events, and one or two safe videos.
